(A) DO THE NUMERIC COLUMNS ALIGN WHEN READ STRAIGHT DOWN?

  MODEL LIST — YES. Measured mechanically from the width-140/30d render above:
  every one of the 8 model rows ends at exactly column 136. The figures are
  right-aligned to a shared end column, which is why '$90.73' and '$0 · 210 req'
  both finish in the same place despite very different lengths.

  STAT STRIP — YES, but on a DIFFERENT and correct rule. The strip is a grid of
  EQUAL-WIDTH COLUMNS, so each figure sits at the same offset inside its column
  and consecutive figures are spaced by exactly one column plus one gutter.
  At width 140 that reads:
      requests    65,460    cost        $112.36   error rate  2.01%     cache share 95.9%
  — even intervals, which is the property the eye uses.

  What is deliberately NOT claimed: the four figures do NOT share one absolute
  column, and cannot. Cell i begins at i x (column + gutter), so only the row's
  LAST cell reaches the right edge. Right-aligning every figure to a single
  absolute column is possible only for a row holding ONE cell, which is the model
  list's shape and not a strip's. An earlier version of this screen joined whole
  cells with three spaces and let them fall where they fell, which put the figures
  at columns 11, 27, 51 and 73 — no two comparable. That is fixed and tested.

  the database contains ZERO requests whose spend could not be priced. Every
  model shown at 24h is genuinely free-tier with an explicit all-zero price card

Sections 1–8 are per-SCREEN renders and every one of them shows a body with no
frame around it, which is how the original complaint went unverified: the tab
strip the user actually navigates with, the border the frame actually draws, the
single `├` divider between body and footer, and the footer hints were never
captured COMPOSED together. What follows is that capture, four frames of forty
rows each, ANSI stripped. `StatsPanel.render(width)` is a pure function of
state, so this is not a screenshot — it is the panel's own output, byte for
byte, at two widths and on two payloads.

TWO CASES, and why both:
  A. A fixture-shaped payload (the panel's own test fixture: two cost buckets,
     1,200 requests, `$42.03`). Deterministic, reviewable, and re-runnable by
     anyone: same input, same rows. THIS is the case a successor re-runs to
     check a renderer change.
  B. `~/.omp/stats.db` (325,238,784 bytes, mtime Oct 3 12:15 (unchanged before and after the read)), read through the SAME `fetchFor` seam the
     panel loads through, range `30d` — `$112.36`, 65,784 requests, 50 unpriced,
     live token mix. Read-only; the database was not written (md5-checked
     before and after). What a fixture cannot show — a real bucket axis, real
     unpriced counts beside the figures they qualify, the tab strip with eight
     live tabs — is here.

CHECKED BEFORE APPENDING, BY MEASUREMENT (cells, not bytes):
  A@100 — 40 rows, widest 100, exactly 1 divider, OVER WIDE: none
  A@60  — 40 rows, widest 60,  exactly 1 divider, OVER WIDE: none
  B@100 — 40 rows, widest 100, exactly 1 divider, OVER WIDE: none
  B@60  — 40 rows, widest 60,  exactly 1 divider, OVER WIDE: none

WHAT TO LOOK AT, AND WHY:
  - The title carries the RANGE (`Stats · 30 days`); the screen name lives in
    the STRIP, not the title (F23 §3.3).
  - At 60 the strip collapses to one-cell shorts — `⬢  $  ▣  ≡  ⚠  …` — while
    the active tab keeps its full label. The collapse order (farthest from the
    active tab first) is TabBar's, not ours.
  - The footer names the keys the panel binds and nothing else
    (`↑/↓ scroll · ←/→ screen · r range · s sync · ⎋/q close`), and the two
    tests in `test/panel.test.ts` starting "the composed frame" pin exactly
    this.
  - The stat grid is the web's `Stat` shape: label row, value row, hint row,
    spark row. A label of any length cannot touch its value.
  - A cost tile never prints a bare `$0.00` for unmeasured spend (`$112.36`
    over `50 unpriced` in B; `N/A` where the spend has no price).
  - No section rule anywhere in any body (G5); the one `├` row is the body's
    only divider, `PanelDivider` between body and footer (G6).

13) $ bun scripts/probe-frame.ts — the WEB-SHELL CHROME, three widths
==============================================================================

The frame the web dashboard has and this panel had not: a topbar
(`omp/stats` + live chip + the range segment) over a sidebar COLUMN of
grouped nav rows (`icon + label + G <letter>`). `src/tui/chrome.ts` ports
Shell.tsx / nav.ts / LiveChip.tsx; `src/tui/panel.ts` composes it and the
topbar replaces the strip row. Below the sidebar width the nav is the
strip row again (TabBar collapses to one-cell TAB_SHORT); below the
chrome height there is no nav row at all.

CHECKED BEFORE APPENDING, BY MEASUREMENT (cells, not bytes):
  FRAME@100 — 40 rows, widest 100, 1 divider(s), OVERWIDE: 0
  FRAME@60 — 40 rows, widest 60, 1 divider(s), OVERWIDE: 0
  FRAME@40 — 40 rows, widest 40, 1 divider(s), OVERWIDE: 0

WHAT TO LOOK AT, AND WHY:
  - 100: the sidebar is a COLUMN. Three dim headings (Usage / Activity /
    Insights) and one row per drawable screen, each with its G-letter. The
    active row reuses the tab strip's luminance step (selectedBg + bold).
  - 60: no column. The nav is the strip row, active tab full-width, neighbours
    collapsed to their one-cell glyphs; the body keeps the full width.
  - Both: the topbar holds brand + live chip + all six ranges on ONE row, and
    the frame still carries exactly one divider (G6) and no body rule (G5).
  - The Live chip is the settled state; press s and the topbar gains a second
    row — the progress line, determinate on ingest with a known total and
    indeterminate on scan/rollup, which report no denominator.
